Two research threads to connect

Can we understand water across scales, from remotely sensed reservoir levels to flooding experienced along a stream? Your dissertation could help turn that question into practical, testable science.

  • Reservoirs viewed from space. Test ways to combine satellite imagery and in-water gauges to track reservoir levels and change, supporting drought resilience and dam safety.
  • Hazards experienced on the ground. Bring stream, coastal and weather sensors together with camera imagery and historical event records to understand locally meaningful flood conditions.
  • Methods that hold up. Develop reproducible Python, geospatial and AI analyses; validate estimates against observations; quantify uncertainty and publish findings.
  • Science with people. Collaborate with researchers, agencies and communities to understand what information is useful for decisions and how best to communicate it.

Who would thrive here?

  • A strong foundation. Bachelor's or master's preparation in hydrology, geoscience, environmental / civil engineering, remote sensing, computer / data science, geography or a related field.
  • Quantitative curiosity. Some programming and data analysis experience, preferably Python, and genuine interest in water and geospatial questions.
  • A research mindset. Willingness to learn, ask questions, test assumptions, communicate clearly and build an original dissertation with practical value.
  • Great extras. Satellite imagery, machine learning, computer vision, field instrumentation, hydrologic modeling or science communication. You don't need all of these.
Beyond the screen

New analyses of reservoir water levels, sensor and event datasets, scientific papers, prototype monitoring approaches and better ways to communicate environmental risk.
