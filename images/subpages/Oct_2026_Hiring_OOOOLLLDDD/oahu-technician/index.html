Hawaiʻi Mesonet
Field Technician

Love electronics, field science, and figuring out why a system stopped working? Help maintain the scientific instruments that make Hawaiʻi's weather data possible.

The work

You'll join a small technical team keeping automated weather stations working across Hawaiʻi. Expect a mix of field trips, instrumentation, remote diagnostics and practical problem-solving.

  • Keep stations running. Install, maintain, calibrate and repair weather sensors, Campbell-style dataloggers, solar power systems and cellular communications.
  • Figure out what went wrong. Use live observations and site visits to investigate failed sensors, power problems, communications outages and missing data.
  • Make the network better. Work with the team on datalogger programs, equipment improvements and automated data quality checks.
  • Make fieldwork happen. Plan station visits, maintain equipment and calibration records, coordinate with site hosts, and help guide student field assistants.

Who we're looking for

  • Training and experience. Bachelor's degree in atmospheric, environmental, geographic or natural resource science, engineering or a closely related field; 1–3 years of relevant field experience.
  • Real technical aptitude. Comfort with tools, improvised repairs, computer software, and basic analysis and quality assessment of environmental time-series data.
  • Independent field judgment. Organized, communicative and confident working in remote settings; valid driver's license and ability to drive a manual-transmission four-wheel-drive vehicle on rough roads.
  • Bonus experience. Campbell Scientific / CRBasic, environmental telemetry, sensor wiring, solar power, programming, remote deployments, work at height, or training students.

*Salary range and position description are from the October 20, 2026 RCUH recruitment draft (Position 00051699). The eventual published listing controls terms, eligibility and closing dates.

The field reality

Bring your boots.

Work involves frequent interisland and sometimes overnight trips to remote weather stations, often under difficult conditions.

  • Hiking on uneven terrain and carrying equipment up to 50 pounds
  • Working outdoors in changing weather and on ladders or towers up to approximately 100 feet
  • Occasional helicopter access and remote-site camping
  • Driving four-wheel-drive vehicles on unpaved roads

Especially useful

Hands-on problem solvers who enjoy tinkering, troubleshooting equipment and keeping scientific measurements dependable.
