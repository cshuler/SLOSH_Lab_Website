Two halves. One unusual job.

50% MesonetField systems, sensors, dataloggers & telemetry
50% ResearchAI, cameras, stream monitoring & hydrologic science

This is a research position for a scientist who can also work with real equipment. Someone comfortable writing Python one day and troubleshooting a Campbell Scientific datalogger the next.

  • Operate research-grade stations. Work with the Hawaiʻi Mesonet team to install, maintain and troubleshoot weather stations, solar power, sensors and remote communications systems.
  • Make observations reliable. Investigate field hardware and software problems, improve station programming, and help evaluate environmental data quality.
  • Build smarter monitoring tools. Develop AI-assisted hazard awareness, edge-camera systems, computer vision for stream stage and flow, and real-time hydrologic sensing approaches.
  • Do original science. Write code, design field experiments, evaluate uncertainty, collaborate with partners and publish results.

Who we're looking for

  • A research-minded hydrologist. PhD-level expertise in hydrology, water resources, environmental / civil engineering, geoscience, atmospheric science or a closely related technical field.
  • Someone who can code. Strong quantitative skills, especially Python and data analysis, plus curiosity and independence in research.
  • Someone who can make instruments work. Hands-on experience with field sensors and datalogger programming or troubleshooting. Campbell Scientific / CRBasic skills are a particular advantage.
  • Useful extras. AI/ML, computer vision, cameras, edge computing, GIS, telemetry, stream gauging and designing field experiments. No one is expected to know everything on day one.
The person we'd love to meet

A scientist who likes the messy parts.

Someone who enjoys the outdoor challenges of remote stations, but also wants to develop ideas, test algorithms, write papers and push environmental sensing forward.

Where the work leads

Better monitoring of streams and environmental hazards, alongside a statewide weather-observation network used by forecasters, researchers and water-resource practitioners.

Research directions and job structure are being developed. Exact duties, funding, appointment term and qualifications require confirmation.
